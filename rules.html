Verbs

Verb types

Nouns

Possessive endings

Feminine -a becomes -t- before an ending: magana, maganti; khala, khalti.

After a vowel, my is -ya and his is -h: khu, khuya, khuh. bba and mmi are irregular: bba, bbak, bbah; mmi, mmek, mmu.

Where endings sound natural: family, body parts and a few everyday words (smiti, dari, ktabi). For most things you own, people use dyal. Family words take only the ending; nobody says l-bba dyali.

The article and dyal

the is l- before most letters: l-ktab, l-bit, l-kelb.

Before t d r z s sh n l j (and their emphatic forms) it copies the first letter instead: d-dar, s-sarut, t-tebla, sh-shems, z-zenqa. Arabic script always writes ال.

dyal goes after the noun with the: l-ktab dyali (my book), d-dar dyalha (her house), l-ktab dyal Amina (Amina's book). dyali, dyalek and so on also mean mine, yours.

Gender and agreement

Nouns ending in -a are usually feminine; others are masculine. Feminine anyway: body parts that come in pairs (yedd, 3in, rjel, wden), dar, shems.

Adjectives follow the noun and agree with it: ktab kbir (a big book), dar kbira (a big house).

With the, both take it: d-dar l-kbira, the big house. Only on the noun, it is a sentence: d-dar kbira, the house is big.

Plurals

Nouns: many change their inside vowels (ktab, ktub; dar, dyur; weld, wlad; bab, biban). Others add -at, especially feminine and borrowed words (tilifun, tilifunat; khala, khalat). Learn each plural with its noun.

Adjectives: add -in (mzyan, mzyanin; 3iyan, 3iyanin) or swap the long i for a (kbir, kbar; jdid, jdad; sghir, sghar). Colors add -in in the book (k7el, k7lin); you will also hear k7el, ku7el.

A plural noun takes the plural adjective, even for things: dyur kbar, big houses.
